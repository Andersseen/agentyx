# Design reference research

Use this when a creative direction needs grounding and the team lacks a reference. Research ideas,
not pages: the goal is principles you can translate, never a layout to reproduce.

## Search by concept

Do not search for the best sites in the product's category; that returns the genre's defaults.
Instead name the concepts behind the product and look at fields that already solved similar
problems. A routing product might look at signal systems, patch panels, topology maps, wiring
diagrams and operator consoles. A publishing tool might look at print production and editorial
systems. A component library might look at catalogs, material samples and assembly instructions.

## Extract, do not copy

For each reference, write down only:

- the principle it relies on;
- how it organizes space and rhythm;
- the metaphor or vocabulary worth borrowing;
- what its type does and why;
- what does not transfer.

Keep a handful of references. Do not reproduce distinctive layouts, illustrations, copy or branding,
and respect licensing for anything you reuse directly.

## Work without network access

When browsing is unavailable, use the repository: existing brand assets, documentation, product
output and screenshots, plus reasoning from the concept fields above. Say which references came from
research and which from reasoning. Never claim to have viewed a source you did not open.

## Bound the effort

Time-box research to what changes a decision. Feed the findings into the creative direction, then
stop researching; more references rarely improve a direction already chosen.

## Stop condition

Stop when you can state two or three principles the design will borrow, and one thing it will
deliberately do differently from the references.
