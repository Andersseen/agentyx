# Creative direction

Use this before a meaningful new website, landing page or redesign where expression matters. Skip it
for routine product screens and for small edits inside an established look; there, preserve the
existing brand and design system.

## Extract the brief

Write a few lines for yourself, grounded in the repository, its README and real content, not in what
sites of this kind usually look like:

- what the product does and who it is for;
- the one thing a visitor must understand or feel;
- the product's own vocabulary: its nouns, verbs, artifacts, data shapes and physical analogies;
- required tone, density and level of expression;
- constraints: existing brand, tokens, components, performance, accessibility, supported browsers.

## Explore before committing

Generate two or three directions that differ in kind, not in color: for example editorial and
typographic, technical and diagrammatic, utilitarian and dense, calm and architectural, kinetic and
playful. These are examples of difference, not a menu. For each, note the central visual idea, what
the first screen shows, and what it would cost to build.

Pick the strongest by asking which one could only belong to this product. Drop directions that need
dependencies, assets or content the project does not have.

## Define a small visual grammar

Before implementing, fix the rules the whole page will obey:

- one organizing motif drawn from the product's vocabulary;
- type roles and how scale is used;
- a restrained color palette with semantic roles, reusing existing tokens when they exist;
- spacing and alignment logic, including where density rises and falls;
- how the motif behaves on narrow screens;
- what is deliberately absent.

## Keep it honest

Visuals must show real product facts. Do not invent features, metrics, logos or testimonials to fill
a composition. Keep roadmap ideas visually separate from shipped behavior.

## Stop condition

Exploration is internal. Share the chosen direction in a short paragraph, mentioning rejected
directions only when the user needs the trade-off. Do not iterate on concepts after implementation
starts unless the grammar proves unworkable.
